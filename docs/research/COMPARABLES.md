
# SuperMonk market research: international comparables

Context: SuperMonk (Cosmo Local hackathon, Chiang Mai, 2026-09-27) lets newcomers invite a monk
for a house blessing, shop opening, memorial, monk chat or meditation, with a **suggested
donation, never a price**. Spec: `PLANS/SUPERMONK_MVP_PLAN.md`.

Every number carries its year and a source. "Unverified" = found only in a blog or company page
without a second source. Sections marked **not completed** had sub-searches still running at the
18:50 UTC cut-off on 2026-09-26; findings are limited to what was independently verified.

## 1. Japan: Obosan-bin (お坊さん便), Minrevi / Yoriso

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| What it is | Flat-fee monk dispatch for memorial services (法事・法要) outside funerals: chanting + short sermon at the customer's home or grave. Booked online, monk assigned from a partner pool. |
| Launched | Own site May 2013 (Minrevi press release, 2015) [1]. Listed on Amazon.co.jp 2015-12-08 [1][2]. |
| By whom | 株式会社みんれび (Minrevi), Tokyo, later renamed 株式会社よりそう (Yoriso) [3]. |
| Price model | 35,000 yen tax-incl, nationwide flat, "no extra charges": includes chanting/sermon, meal allowance (御膳料), transport (お車代), gratuity (心づけ); +10,000 yen for a second location such as the grave (2015) [1]. Later added "おきもち支払い" (customer chooses the amount within a band after the service) (2019) [3]. |
| Traction | Orders up 300% year-on-year by 2014 on the original site (company claim, 2015) [1]. Inquiries in FY2018 about 13x FY2014 (company figure reported by ITmedia search summary, 2019; not re-verified on the page) [3]. No absolute order counts published. |
| What happened | Japan Buddhist Federation (全日本仏教会, JBF) chairman's statement 2015-12-24 [4]. Yoriso announced 2019-10-24 it would stop selling on Amazon; the service continued on yoriso.com [3][5]. |
| Controversy | JBF chairman Saito Meisei, 2015-12-24: "お布施は、サービスの対価ではありません" (a donation is not payment for a service); "『戒名』『法名』も商品ではない"; the service "宗教行為をサービスとして商品にしている" (turns a religious act into a commercial product) [4]. Company's stated reason for leaving Amazon, 2019: on a large EC site "the rite itself appeared to be 'listed' as a product" and the limited page format "could not convey the role of memorial services", spreading misunderstanding [3][5]. A JBF official welcomed the change of policy toward "supporting bereaved hearts" (Kyoto Shimbun, 2019-10-25) [5]. |
| Lesson for SuperMonk | The objection was never to booking a monk online; it was to (a) a **fixed price displayed as a product** and (b) the **platform's framing** (an Amazon "Add to cart" button). Yoriso survived by moving the touchpoint to its own site and adding a pay-what-you-feel band. SuperMonk's "invite + suggested donation" and no cart is exactly the post-2019 Yoriso design. Expect the Sangha's objection to target the intermediary's cut, not the app. |

## 2. India: priest-booking and puja apps

**Status: partially completed.** Sri Mandir verified; Utsav / OmPooja / Book A Pandit / sector
funding totals not completed.

| Field | Sri Mandir (AppsForBharat) |
|---|---|
| What it is | Devotional app: online puja performed by temple priests on the user's behalf (video proof), chadhava offerings, prasad delivery, temple audio/video. Not a home-visit priest service. |
| Launched | Company founded 2020, Bengaluru; founder-CEO Prashant Sachan (2025) [6]. |
| Price model | Fixed prices per puja/offering listed in-app (paid service, not donation). |
| Funding | Series B $18M (Sept 2024); Series C $20M / Rs 175 cr led by Susquehanna Asia VC with Fundamentum, Elevation Capital, Peak XV, announced 2025-06-30/07-01; total raised >$50M (2025) [6][7][8]. |
| Traction | 40M+ downloads; 5.2M online pujas in the trailing 12 months across 70+ temples; 1.2M users who performed a puja in 12 months; ~20% of demand from US, UK, UAE, Canada, Australia, NZ (company figures via Entrackr, 2025) [6]. |
| Revenue | FY24 operating revenue Rs 18.53 cr (5x YoY), loss Rs 39 cr (FY23 loss Rs 44.97 cr) (Entrackr, 2025) [6]. |
| What happened | Still scaling; Series C earmarked for 20+ temple towns (Varanasi, Ayodhya, Ujjain, Haridwar), logistics hubs for prasad, AI features (2025) [7][8]. |
| Controversy | Not researched to a primary source in this pass (temple-trust objections and ASCI complaints: not completed). |
| Lesson for SuperMonk | The Indian model that raised >$50M is **remote ritual + physical goods**, priced openly, with a diaspora (~20%) paying from abroad. It shows a large paying audience for "ritual done for me" but Hindu temple priests are commercially remunerated in a way Theravada monks are not; SuperMonk cannot copy the price list, only the diaspora/newcomer demand signal. |

## 3. Korea: Templestay (Cultural Corps of Korean Buddhism, Jogye Order)

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| What it is | Overnight temple-life programme (chanting, meditation, tea with a monk, temple food) at Jogye Order temples; state-supported cultural tourism, not a monk-dispatch service. |
| Launched | 2002 (FIFA World Cup); started with 33 temples and about 2,500 participants (Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-01-18) [9]. |
| By whom | 대한불교조계종 한국불교문화사업단 (Cultural Corps of Korean Buddhism) [9]. |
| Price model | Programme fees per temple; a 2026 foreigner campaign priced 1 night/2 days at 30,000 won at 19 temples, September 2026 only (Asia Economy, 2026-07-31) [10]. Standard fee ranges: not completed. |
| Scale | 2025: 158 temples, 293,704 Koreans + 55,515 foreigners = 349,219 participants, +5.1% vs 2024, record high (Cultural Corps figures via Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-01-18) [9]. Foreigners first passed 50,000 in 2018 (53,291, PyeongChang Olympics year), collapsed during COVID, exceeded 2018 again in 2025 [9]. Cumulative 2002–2025: 4,184,373 participants [9]. Foreigner share 2025: 15.9% (55,515/349,219, computed). |
| Religion of participants | 2025: 52.5% of Korean and 43.4% of foreign participants reported no religion (Asia Economy, 2026-07-31) [10]. |
| Government funding | Annual state subsidy amount: not completed (needs 문화체육관광부 budget line). |
| Controversy | Not completed. |
| Lesson for SuperMonk | The most successful Buddhist-encounter product in Asia is **run by the Sangha itself, subsidised, and sold as culture/wellness, not religion**: over half of Korean participants are non-religious. Foreign demand is real (55k/yr) and price-sensitive (30,000 won promos sell out). SuperMonk should position as cultural navigation for newcomers and seek temple/Sangha ownership of the listing, not a private marketplace. |

## Cross-cutting lessons (from verified sections only)

| Lesson | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Display a donation band, never a product price | JBF 2015 statement targeted "定額" and "商品"; Yoriso's fix was pay-what-you-feel (2019) [3][4] |
| Keep the booking surface owned by, or endorsed by, the religious body | Templestay (Sangha-run) reached 349k/yr (2025) [9]; Obosan-bin lost its channel when the body objected (2019) [5] |
| Non-religious and foreign users are the growth segment | 52.5% of Korean Templestay users non-religious (2025) [10]; ~20% of Sri Mandir demand from abroad (2025) [6] |
| Intermediary economics are the flashpoint | Neither JBF nor Yoriso disputed that monks were paid; the fight was over a company selling the rite [4][5] |
