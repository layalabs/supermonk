
# SuperMonk market research: competitors and business model

Oppo's section of the market research. Fab merges it into `RESEARCH/SUPERMONK_MARKET_RESEARCH.md`. Researched 2026-09-26/27 (UTC). Three parallel research passes (Thai services; foreign platforms and marketplaces; donation norms and revenue models), then spot-checked by Oppo against the primary pages. **Labels:** VERIFIED / SOURCED = read on the cited page. SNIPPET = from a search result only (the page blocked fetching). UNVERIFIED = could not confirm. ESTIMATE = our inference, arithmetic shown. BLOG = a lead, not a source.

## Key findings

| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nobody offers what SuperMonk does: English booking of a monk to a foreigner's home, shop, vehicle or memorial in Chiang Mai.** | Every competitor below either takes you *to* a temple on its schedule (Temple Stairway, MCU Monk Chat, tour marketplaces), or serves Thais in Thai (Phalangsattha, Merit Event, Boonumpar) |
| 2 | **The Thai supply side already exists, but it sells fixed-price packages in Thai.** Chiang Mai organisers charge 6,900–25,900 THB for 5 monks (Phalangsattha) and 7,999–12,999 THB for 3–9 monks (Merit Event / nganboon.com), with the monks' envelopes paid by the host on top | phalangsattha.com/tamboonban and nganboon.com/event_detail.php?id=4, both re-checked by Oppo 2026-09-27 |
| 3 | **The closest digital competitor is Temple Stairway**: "+500 authentic Buddhist experiences", mostly donation-based, one retreat "From 3,900 THB", no disclosed fee, and no home or shop ceremonies. A judge will probably name it, and it could add home blessings | templestairway.com, re-checked 2026-09-27. Its "50+ temples" claim is UNVERIFIED (not on the homepage today) |
| 4 | **Tour marketplaces sell monk contact as a ticket.** Example: Airbnb sunrise at Doi Suthep with an ex-monk, ฿1,900 per guest, 4.96 from 665 reviews. The platforms take about 20–30% (Airbnb Experiences 20%, VERIFIED) | airbnb.com/experiences/4009971; airbnb.com/help/article/1857 |
| 5 | **AI monk bots (US$4–8/month) cover "monk chat" only**, and all of them say they are not real monks | bhante.net, myaimonk.com |
| 6 | **Monks must never be charged, and SuperMonk must never touch the envelope.** The monastic rules forbid monks handling money, and a lay steward (ไวยาวัจกร) receives it. Since 1 Jan 2026, gifts to temples are tax-deductible only through the Revenue Department's e-Donation system | Thai PBS, thaipbs.or.th/news/content/355499 (re-checked); rd.go.th |
| 7 | **Envelopes follow custom, not a rate card.** About 100–300 THB per ordinary monk, about 500 for an abbot, 300–500 per monk per funeral night. A house blessing with 5–9 monks costs about 14,000 THB all in (envelopes, food, offerings) in a typical published budget, and up to 28,500 THB in a larger example for about 50 guests | See section C |
| 8 | **Japan's Obosan-bin is the warning precedent**: a fixed ¥35,000 dispatch fee that includes the donation, condemned by the Japan Buddhist Federation in Dec 2015 as treating a donation like payment for a service. SuperMonk's rule "suggested donation, paid to the temple, never through us" is the positioning that avoids this | Section C; Fab covers the full precedent |

## Revenue models that never charge a monk (summary; details and arithmetic in section C)

| Model | Who pays | Gross per month (ESTIMATE) | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Small booking fee (199 THB) plus an optional tip, paid by the host | Host | ~16,740 THB | Fee on a merit act may feel wrong; keep it clearly a coordination fee |
| B. 15% commission on ceremony kits and catering from local vendors | Vendors | ~18,000 THB | Kit quality and delivery; kits cost ~120–299 THB each online today |
| C. Monthly retainer plus per-event fee from relocation agents, condo developers and hotels | Businesses | ~40,000 THB | Sales cycle; needs 2–3 anchor partners |
| Rejected: a cut of temple donation drives | Temples | 0 | It takes a share of money given to monks, the same objection as Obosan-bin |

All volumes and prices behind these are assumptions, labelled in section C. Together, about 74,700 THB/month at the assumed volumes.

## What to say when a judge asks "doesn't X already do this?"

| Judge names | Answer in one line |
|---|---|
| Temple Stairway | It books you into a temple's schedule. We bring a monk to your life event, in English, and prepare you for it |
| MCU Monk Chat | A partner, not a rival. We route people there with the right hours and etiquette |
| Airbnb / GetYourGuide tours | A ticket for tourists, with a 20–30% cut. We're for residents' own ceremonies, and the monk gets the whole donation |
| Thai organisers (Phalangsattha, Merit Event) | They're Thai-only packages. They could become our fulfilment partners |
| AI monk bots | They replace the monk. We get you to a real one |
| Obosan-bin | They fixed a price on a donation and were condemned for it. We never price or touch the donation |

### Gaps SuperMonk fills

- **Language.** Every organiser and shop checked is Thai only. monkchat.net, a temple program, is the only English-language monk service found. The only app with English is เด่นดวง, and it does not book monks.
- **Donation framing instead of a price.** Organisers sell fixed packages from about 5,000 to 36,900 THB that bundle catering, equipment and monks. Nothing tells a foreigner what an appropriate donation for the monks themselves would be.
- **Small, single-purpose visits.** No product was found for a single monk blessing a vehicle, a short home chat, or meditation guidance at home. Organisers run 5-9 monk events with guests.
- **Etiquette and preparation.** No service found gives foreigners guidance on dress, seating, offerings, the sai sin cord, or what to say.
- **Matching by area and language.** Temples take bookings in their own way (for example Wat Nyanavesakavan: LINE, 45 days ahead, 5 monks at most). No aggregator was found that matches a nomad's condo area in Chiang Mai to a nearby temple or an English-speaking monk.
- **Donations work for foreigners.** e-Donation needs a Thai bank app, and its tax benefit is for Thai taxpayers. A foreigner without a Thai bank account has no easy digital way to donate. The foreign-card angle is my inference and was not verified.
- **Monk chat outside temple hours.** Existing monk chats happen only at the temple at set times.

### Gaps (where SuperMonk is uncontested, or where evidence is thin)

- **No English-language service was found that brings a monk to a foreigner's home, shop, vehicle or memorial in Chiang Mai.** Supply exists (Thai caterers like Boonumpar, Bangkok-focused) but is Thai-only. The demand path today is "ask a Thai friend/landlord" (Tieland to Thailand blog: the invitation came via personal connections).
- Temple Stairway is the closest digital player, but it is **temple-side and schedule-driven** (retreats, monk chat), mostly donation-based, with no disclosed revenue model. It could add home blessings, which is a competitive risk worth naming.
- Tour OTAs sell monk contact as a **tour ticket** (฿1,900 / US$25–55) and take 20–30%. The blessing is bundled and not requested. SuperMonk's suggested-donation model avoids that take, but has no disclosed monetisation.
- AI monk bots (US$4–8/mo) cover "monk chat" only and disclaim being real monks. Nothing verified matches by language or area to real monks.
- Obousan-bin proves people will pay a fixed fee for a dispatched priest (¥35k), but it is also controversial. The Japan Buddhist Federation objection in 2016 is UNVERIFIED in this session and recalled from press. A Thai sangha-etiquette risk (monks and money) should be anticipated.
- **Evidence gaps:** GYG, Viator, Klook and TripAdvisor prices and review counts could not be fetched (403). Wat Suan Dok retreat prices conflict between sources and the operator site shows none. The Wat Sri Suphan and Wat Umong schedules come only from blogs. Klook commission was not researched. Temple Stairway's operator and fee model are undisclosed.

#### Monastic discipline and money

| Rule | What it says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Vinaya, Nissaggiya Pācittiya 18 (โกสิยวรรค สิกขาบทที่ ๘, the rule on accepting gold and silver) | A monk who takes money, has it taken for him, or consents to it being set aside for him commits an offence requiring forfeiture | Primary: Tipiṭaka vol. 2, 84000.org; commentary: dhammahome.com |

| Thai Sangha law | The ไวยาวัจกร (temple steward) is appointed under กฎมหาเถรสมาคม ฉบับที่ 18 (พ.ศ. 2536 / 1993). The steward receives money and turns it into the four requisites. Monks should not even direct where money goes. Thai civil law has no matching ban | Thai PBS, 19 Oct 2024 |
| Revenue Department, 2026 | From 1 Jan 2026 (2569), gifts to temples count for tax deduction only if made through e-Donation. Paper receipts (ใบอนุโมทนาบัตร) no longer qualify. **What this means for the product:**
- The envelope stays cash, handed over by the host at the ceremony, or goes by transfer or e-Donation QR to the **temple's** account or steward.
- SuperMonk never holds, collects or splits the donation.
- For foreigners paying tax in Thailand, the e-Donation QR is also a selling point.

### Three revenue models that never charge a monk

All volumes and prices below are **ASSUMPTIONS** for a Chiang Mai pilot, around month 12, not sourced figures. THB throughout. Processing fees are not modelled; PromptPay QR is about 0% for the payer.

#### Model 1: User booking fee plus optional tip, charged to the host only

- **Price:** flat 199 THB per ceremony booking. This covers coordination, translation, the etiquette brief and the reminder.
- **Monk chat and meditation:** free to book, with an optional tip.
- **Tip:** optional on top, in the Give.asia and GoFundMe style.
- **Precedent:** Airbnb Services charges a flat $6 minimum; Give.asia and GoFundMe run on tips.

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Ceremony bookings per month | 60 |
| Fee | 199 |
| Monk chat / meditation bookings | 100 |
| Tip rate × average tip | 30% × 100 THB |

**Arithmetic:**
- Fees: 60 × 199 = **11,940**
- Tips: (60 + 100) × 0.30 × 100 = **4,800**
- **Gross: 16,740 THB per month**

The envelope (for example 9 monks × 300 = 2,700) passes untouched, host to temple steward.

#### Model 2: Ceremony-kit and catering margin, paid by local vendors

- **What it is:** SuperMonk sells the offering kit (สังฆทาน baskets, flowers, holy thread, and food for the monks) through Chiang Mai sangha-goods shops and caterers.
- **Who pays:** the vendor pays the commission. Monks and temples pay nothing.
- **Precedent:** SmartPuja bundles supplies; the Klook commission band is 15–25%.
- **Sizing basis:** Kapook 2020 put ceremonial offerings at 3,000 THB for 5 monks. Monks' food is part of the 8,436 food line.

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Ceremonies per month | 60 |
| Share that order a kit | 50% |
| Average kit + monk-food order | 4,000 THB |
| Vendor commission | 15% |

**Arithmetic:**
- Orders: 60 × 0.5 = 30
- GMV: 30 × 4,000 = 120,000
- **Gross: 120,000 × 0.15 = 18,000 THB per month**

**Risk:** a margin on offerings could look like "selling merit". Show the vendor price and the commission openly.

#### Model 3: B2B concierge for relocation agents, condo developers and hotels

- **What it is:** partners pay a monthly retainer to offer a "Thai blessing" as a move-in, handover or opening perk, plus a per-event coordination fee.
- **Where the donation goes:** the partner or client still gives the envelope directly to the temple.

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Partners | 5 |
| Retainer | 5,000 per month |
| Coordinated events | 10 per month |
| Coordination fee | 1,500 per event |

**Arithmetic:**
- Retainers: 5 × 5,000 = 25,000
- Events: 10 × 1,500 = 15,000
- **Gross: 40,000 THB per month**

**Combined illustrative gross: 16,740 + 18,000 + 40,000 = 74,740 THB per month (ASSUMPTION).**

#### Rejected: sponsorship or cut from temple donation drives

Any share of temple donations is effectively a cut of money given to the Sangha. It repeats the Obosan-bin criticism (a donation is not payment for a service) and conflicts with the ไวยาวัจกร principle. A listing fee paid by temples was rejected for the same reason.
